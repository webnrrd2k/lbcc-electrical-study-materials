## 1. Bender marks

<details><summary><b>2.</b> Which bender mark is used for the back of a 90° (back-to-back 90s)?</summary><br>The star, at the back heel of the shoe.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>3.</b> Which bender mark is used for the center bend of a 3-point saddle?</summary><br>The notch, rim or teardrop at the center of the bend.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>4.</b> Which mark do you use for the two bends of an offset?</summary><br>The arrow, for both marks.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>5.</b> Which marks do you use for the outer bends of a 3-point saddle?</summary><br>The arrow.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>6.</b> How do you check bend angle with a hand bender?</summary><br>Read the degree scale cast on the side of the shoe, or check with a torpedo level or protractor.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>7.</b> Which EMT shoe bends 1/2 in. rigid (RMC/IMC)?</summary><br>The 3/4 in. EMT shoe. The 1 in. EMT shoe bends 3/4 in. rigid.<br><br></details>

## 2. 90° stubs, deductions and gain

<details><summary><b>8.</b> How do you find the mark for a 90° stub-up?</summary><br>Mark = desired stub height − bender deduction (take-up). Line the mark up with the arrow.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>9.</b> 90° deduction for 1/2 in. EMT?</summary><br>5 in.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>10.</b> 90° deduction for 3/4 in. EMT?</summary><br>6 in.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>11.</b> 90° deduction for 1 in. EMT?</summary><br>8 in.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>12.</b> 90° deduction for 1-1/4 in. EMT?</summary><br>11 in.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>13.</b> 90° deductions for 1/2, 3/4 and 1 in. rigid (RMC)?</summary><br>6 in., 8 in. and 11 in. Rigid uses the next-size-up EMT shoe, so it takes that shoe&#x27;s deduction.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>14.</b> What is gain?</summary><br>The length saved because a bend takes a curved shortcut. Total pipe needed = leg 1 + leg 2 − gain.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>15.</b> Worked: 12 in. stub with 1/2 in. EMT. Where is the mark?</summary><br>12 − 5 = 7 in. from the end, lined up with the arrow.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>16.</b> Worked: 18 in. stub with 3/4 in. rigid. Where is the mark?</summary><br>18 − 8 = 10 in. from the end, on the arrow.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>17.</b> How do you make the second bend of a back-to-back 90?</summary><br>Measure the outside-to-outside distance from the back of the first 90, line that mark up with the star, hook facing away. No deduction.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>18.</b> Why over-bend rigid conduit slightly?</summary><br>Steel RMC springs back. Over-bend about 2° to 3°.<br><br></details>

## 3. Offsets and shrinkage

<details><summary><b>19.</b> How far apart are the two marks of an offset?</summary><br>Offset height × the multiplier for the bend angle.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>20.</b> Offset multiplier for 10° bends?</summary><br>6.0<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>21.</b> Offset multiplier for 22.5° bends?</summary><br>2.6<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>22.</b> Offset multiplier for 30° bends?</summary><br>2.0<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>23.</b> Offset multiplier for 45° bends?</summary><br>1.4 (exactly 1.414)<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>24.</b> Offset multiplier for 60° bends?</summary><br>1.2 (exactly 1.155)<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>25.</b> What is the multiplier mathematically?</summary><br>The cosecant of the bend angle: 1 ÷ sin(angle). For 30°, 1 ÷ 0.5 = 2.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>26.</b> Shrinkage per inch of offset height for 10°, 22.5°, 30°, 45° and 60° bends?</summary><br>1/16, 3/16, 1/4, 3/8 and 1/2 in. per inch.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>27.</b> When bending toward an obstacle, what do you do with shrinkage?</summary><br>Add it to the distance to the obstacle when placing the first mark.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>28.</b> Worked: 6 in. offset with 30° bends. Mark spacing and shrinkage?</summary><br>6 × 2 = 12 in. between marks. Shrinkage 6 × 1/4 = 1-1/2 in.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>29.</b> Worked: 4 in. offset with 45° bends. Mark spacing?</summary><br>4 × 1.414 ≈ 5-5/8 in.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>30.</b> Why choose a smaller offset angle like 22.5° over 45°?</summary><br>Less shrinkage and easier wire pulling, but the offset takes more length.<br><br></details>

## 4. Saddles and box offsets

<details><summary><b>31.</b> What are the bend angles of a 3-point saddle?</summary><br>22.5° – 45° – 22.5°.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>32.</b> 3-point saddle: where is the center mark?</summary><br>Distance to the center of the obstacle + 3/16 in. per inch of obstacle height. Line it up with the notch or rim.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>33.</b> 3-point saddle: where are the outer marks?</summary><br>2-1/2 in. per inch of obstacle height on each side of the center mark, lined up with the arrow.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>34.</b> Worked: 3-point saddle over a 4 in. pipe, center 30 in. from the end. Marks?</summary><br>Center at 30 + 4 × 3/16 = 30-3/4 in. Outer marks 4 × 2-1/2 = 10 in. each side: 20-3/4 in. and 40-3/4 in.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>35.</b> What is a 4-point saddle?</summary><br>Two offsets back to back, usually 30° or 22.5°, to go over a wide obstacle.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>36.</b> 4-point saddle: list the four marks.</summary><br>1: distance to obstacle + shrinkage. 2: mark 1 + height × multiplier. 3: mark 2 + obstacle width. 4: mark 3 + height × multiplier.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>37.</b> What bends make a box offset?</summary><br>Two 10° bends, usually for a 3/8 or 1/2 in. offset into a box.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>38.</b> Worked: 1/2 in. box offset. How far apart are the marks?</summary><br>0.5 × 6.0 = 3 in. The first mark is 2 to 3 in. from the end.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>39.</b> Box offset and a stub-up on the same piece: which bend first?</summary><br>The box offset first, then measure the stub from that end.<br><br></details>

## 5. NEC rules

<details><summary><b>40.</b> Maximum total degrees of bends between pull points?</summary><br>360°, counting offsets and saddles (NEC 358.26 EMT, 344.26 RMC).<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>41.</b> How many degrees does a 3-point saddle (22.5-45-22.5) add toward the 360° limit?</summary><br>90°.<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>42.</b> EMT securing and support distances?</summary><br>Within 3 ft of every box, cabinet or fitting, and at least every 10 ft (NEC 358.30(A)).<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>43.</b> RMC securing and support distances?</summary><br>Within 3 ft of every box or fitting and every 10 ft; longer spacing is allowed for straight runs with threaded couplings (NEC 344.30).<br><br></details>

<details><summary><b>44.</b> Why does the NEC limit bends between pull points?</summary><br>Every bend adds pulling tension. Too many bends damage the conductor insulation during the pull.<br><br></details>
