Which bender mark do you line up for a standard 90° stub-up?	The arrow (front of the hook).
Which bender mark is used for the back of a 90° (back-to-back 90s)?	The star, at the back heel of the shoe.
Which bender mark is used for the center bend of a 3-point saddle?	The notch, rim or teardrop at the center of the bend.
Which mark do you use for the two bends of an offset?	The arrow, for both marks.
Which marks do you use for the outer bends of a 3-point saddle?	The arrow.
How do you check bend angle with a hand bender?	Read the degree scale cast on the side of the shoe, or check with a torpedo level or protractor.
Which EMT shoe bends 1/2 in. rigid (RMC/IMC)?	The 3/4 in. EMT shoe. The 1 in. EMT shoe bends 3/4 in. rigid.
How do you find the mark for a 90° stub-up?	Mark = desired stub height − bender deduction (take-up). Line the mark up with the arrow.
90° deduction for 1/2 in. EMT?	5 in.
90° deduction for 3/4 in. EMT?	6 in.
90° deduction for 1 in. EMT?	8 in.
90° deduction for 1-1/4 in. EMT?	11 in.
90° deductions for 1/2, 3/4 and 1 in. rigid (RMC)?	6 in., 8 in. and 11 in. Rigid uses the next-size-up EMT shoe, so it takes that shoe's deduction.
What is gain?	The length saved because a bend takes a curved shortcut. Total pipe needed = leg 1 + leg 2 − gain.
Worked: 12 in. stub with 1/2 in. EMT. Where is the mark?	12 − 5 = 7 in. from the end, lined up with the arrow.
Worked: 18 in. stub with 3/4 in. rigid. Where is the mark?	18 − 8 = 10 in. from the end, on the arrow.
How do you make the second bend of a back-to-back 90?	Measure the outside-to-outside distance from the back of the first 90, line that mark up with the star, hook facing away. No deduction.
Why over-bend rigid conduit slightly?	Steel RMC springs back. Over-bend about 2° to 3°.
How far apart are the two marks of an offset?	Offset height × the multiplier for the bend angle.
Offset multiplier for 10° bends?	6.0
Offset multiplier for 22.5° bends?	2.6
Offset multiplier for 30° bends?	2.0
Offset multiplier for 45° bends?	1.4 (exactly 1.414)
Offset multiplier for 60° bends?	1.2 (exactly 1.155)
What is the multiplier mathematically?	The cosecant of the bend angle: 1 ÷ sin(angle). For 30°, 1 ÷ 0.5 = 2.
Shrinkage per inch of offset height for 10°, 22.5°, 30°, 45° and 60° bends?	1/16, 3/16, 1/4, 3/8 and 1/2 in. per inch.
When bending toward an obstacle, what do you do with shrinkage?	Add it to the distance to the obstacle when placing the first mark.
Worked: 6 in. offset with 30° bends. Mark spacing and shrinkage?	6 × 2 = 12 in. between marks. Shrinkage 6 × 1/4 = 1-1/2 in.
Worked: 4 in. offset with 45° bends. Mark spacing?	4 × 1.414 ≈ 5-5/8 in.
Why choose a smaller offset angle like 22.5° over 45°?	Less shrinkage and easier wire pulling, but the offset takes more length.
What are the bend angles of a 3-point saddle?	22.5° – 45° – 22.5°.
3-point saddle: where is the center mark?	Distance to the center of the obstacle + 3/16 in. per inch of obstacle height. Line it up with the notch or rim.
3-point saddle: where are the outer marks?	2-1/2 in. per inch of obstacle height on each side of the center mark, lined up with the arrow.
Worked: 3-point saddle over a 4 in. pipe, center 30 in. from the end. Marks?	Center at 30 + 4 × 3/16 = 30-3/4 in. Outer marks 4 × 2-1/2 = 10 in. each side: 20-3/4 in. and 40-3/4 in.
What is a 4-point saddle?	Two offsets back to back, usually 30° or 22.5°, to go over a wide obstacle.
4-point saddle: list the four marks.	1: distance to obstacle + shrinkage. 2: mark 1 + height × multiplier. 3: mark 2 + obstacle width. 4: mark 3 + height × multiplier.
What bends make a box offset?	Two 10° bends, usually for a 3/8 or 1/2 in. offset into a box.
Worked: 1/2 in. box offset. How far apart are the marks?	0.5 × 6.0 = 3 in. The first mark is 2 to 3 in. from the end.
Box offset and a stub-up on the same piece: which bend first?	The box offset first, then measure the stub from that end.
Maximum total degrees of bends between pull points?	360°, counting offsets and saddles (NEC 358.26 EMT, 344.26 RMC).
How many degrees does a 3-point saddle (22.5-45-22.5) add toward the 360° limit?	90°.
EMT securing and support distances?	Within 3 ft of every box, cabinet or fitting, and at least every 10 ft (NEC 358.30(A)).
RMC securing and support distances?	Within 3 ft of every box or fitting and every 10 ft; longer spacing is allowed for straight runs with threaded couplings (NEC 344.30).
Why does the NEC limit bends between pull points?	Every bend adds pulling tension. Too many bends damage the conductor insulation during the pull.
