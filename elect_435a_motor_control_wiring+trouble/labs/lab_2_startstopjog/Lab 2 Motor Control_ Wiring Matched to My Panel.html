Each TB terminal is a feed-through, so its top and bottom screws are the same point. The orange comb jumpers tie TB4–TB6 into one X4N bus and TB7–TB9 into one 24 V bus. TB2, TB3, TBG2, TBG3, TBL1–3, OL1 97/98, PB3, SS1, PL2 and LS1/LS2 have nothing on them in this lab.

Problems and things to check

1Schematic: JOG skips the STOP button. Rung 7 is fed straight from TB8 on the 24 V bus, not from the far side of STOP. If someone is holding JOG, pressing STOP does nothing. M stays on until JOG is let go (or OL1 trips). In the usual "jog with a control relay" circuit, STOP comes first and feeds both the CR1 rung and the M rung, so STOP always wins. To fix it, feed wire 7 from wire 2 (the load side of STOP) instead of from TB8. Your panel is wired the way the lab sheet shows, so ask your instructor whether this is on purpose.
2The cord neutral is a black wire. Your cord is a flat 3-wire cord where both current-carrying wires are black, and the neutral is marked only by ribs on its jacket. The black on TB1 has to be the ribbed one. If hot and neutral are swapped, CB1 ends up switching the neutral, and the transformer primary stays live even with CB1 off. Check it with the cord plugged in: CB1-1 should read about 120 V to ground, and TB1 about 0 V. Then put white tape on the neutral at both ends. The cord also comes in through a Halex clamp made for NM (Romex) cable. A grip listed for flexible cord is the right part, so check whether your instructor cares.
3Things the lab sheet doesn't make obvious (no change needed). (a) On the 8-pin socket, CR1's "A1/A2" are pins 2 and 7. Its contacts are 1–3 and 8–6, which match the 750-series pinout. (b) The "M 7L4 – 14/22" contact on rung 9 is the contactor's 4th pole (7L4–8T4) used as a holding contact. You reach its bottom end through the overload's 14/22 pass-through terminal, because OL1 covers 8T4. (c) In the same way, the white loop at OL1 "A2" is really the contactor coil's A2. (d) The fuse sizes work: 50 VA ÷ 120 V ≈ 0.42 A primary, so CB1 at 1.5 A is under the 500% allowed for control transformers drawing less than 2 A. 50 VA ÷ 24 V ≈ 2.1 A secondary, which CB2 at 2 A fits.
4Wire 4 is white, but it isn't grounded. Wire 4 runs from the coils to OL1-95, and it sits upstream of the OL contact. When OL1 trips with a coil energized, this "white" wire reads 24 V to ground. NFPA 79 keeps white for grounded conductors only, so strictly wire 4 should be red, or whatever your class uses for control wires. Wires 5 and 11 land straight on the X4N bus, so white is correct for them. This is a fine point, and plenty of lab panels do it the same way.
5The power circuit isn't built yet. Nothing is on TBL1–3 or on M's 1L1/3L2/5L3, so the M contacts and OL1 heaters in the right half of the sheet aren't in your photos yet. When you wire the motor, set OL1's dial to the motor's nameplate full-load current. A ½ HP 3-phase motor is roughly 1–2.5 A depending on voltage, and the dial should read in that range.
6Small housekeeping items. I couldn't see wire-number markers on the conductors. Adding them (1, 2, 3, ... 11) would tie the panel to the sheet in a way anyone can follow. The "TBL1-3" label sits under the CR1 socket rather than under the 3PH terminals. Also check that the door is bonded to the enclosure with a ground jumper, since it holds metal-bodied devices. That jumper isn't in the photos.

Things I couldn't confirm from the photos

I checked the transformer links, both breakers, the TB groups, the overload terminals (95/96/A2/14/22), the contactor (7L4/A1) and the door devices against your photos, and they match the sheet. The one place I couldn't trace every conductor is CR1's socket. There are five red ferrules on top, a red and a white on the bottom, and the relay hides part of it. Since the bottom white can only be wire 4, the bottom row is the coil, so I've shown wire 3 reaching pin 2 from START. Worth checking with a continuity test: pin 1 to START 3; pins 2 and 3 to START 4; pin 8 to TB8 and JOG 3; pin 6 to JOG 4 and M A1.
