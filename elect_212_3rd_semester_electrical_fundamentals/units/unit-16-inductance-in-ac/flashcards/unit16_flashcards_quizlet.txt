What is inductance (L), and what is its unit?	A coil's ability to induce voltage in itself, opposing any change in current. Measured in henrys (H). (1 mH = 0.001 H)
Which loads are treated as inductive?	Anything with a coil: motors, transformers, ballasts, chokes, solenoids, contactor/relay coils. (A single conductor's inductance is tiny and usually ignored)
When does a magnetic field induce a voltage in a coil?	Whenever flux cuts the coil, i.e. whenever the current (and its field) changes. (Unit 13 fact 1)
What is CEMF?	Counter-electromotive force: the induced voltage, which always opposes the applied voltage. (Lenz's law)
What is the induced voltage proportional to?	The RATE OF CHANGE of current, not the amount of current.
What does an inductor oppose?	A CHANGE in current. (Like a flywheel for current)
What is the phase relationship between applied voltage and induced voltage?	180° (exactly opposite).
A coil (6 Ω wire) on 120 VAC draws 0.8 A. How much voltage pushes current through the wire resistance?	E = 0.8 × 6 = 4.8 V. The rest is used overcoming the CEMF.
Same coil: what is the induced voltage? Why not 120 − 4.8?	√(120² − 4.8²) = 119.904 V. The resistive drop and the CEMF are 90° apart, so subtract as vectors. (The book's earlier 120 − 116 = 4 example is only a rough illustration)
A contactor coil reads 6 Ω on an ohmmeter. Does it draw 120/6 = 20 A on 120 VAC?	No. The ohmmeter shows only wire resistance. Reactance limits the AC current to far less.
What is inductive reactance (Xₗ)?	The current-limiting effect of inductance, in ohms. It isn't resistance: it comes from the CEMF and produces no heat.
Formula for Xₗ?	Xₗ = 2πfL (f in Hz, L in H, Xₗ in Ω)
Rearranged reactance formulas?	L = Xₗ / (2πf) and f = Xₗ / (2πL)
What number should you memorize for 60 Hz, and why?	377 (2π × 60 = 376.99). At 60 Hz, Xₗ = 377 × L and L = Xₗ / 377. (Doesn't apply at other frequencies)
How does Ohm's law work with reactance?	Put Xₗ in place of R: I = E / Xₗ, E = I × Xₗ, Xₗ = E / I.
Three factors that set induced voltage (and therefore Xₗ)?	1. Number of turns 2. Strength of the magnetic field 3. Speed of the cutting action (frequency)
What physical features set a coil's inductance?	Number of turns, how close together they are, wire/coil size, and core material (iron ≫ air).
0.8 H on 120 V, 60 Hz. Xₗ and current?	Xₗ = 377 × 0.8 = 301.6 Ω; I = 120 / 301.6 = 0.398 A. (Example 16–1)
36 V, 400 Hz, 0.2 A. Find L.	Xₗ = 36/0.2 = 180 Ω; L = 180 / (2π × 400) = 0.0716 H. (Example 16–2: 377 does NOT apply at 400 Hz)
A coil draws 24 A on 480 V, 60 Hz. Current at 400 Hz?	L doesn't change. Current scales with 1/f: 24 × 60/400 = 3.6 A. (Example 16–3)
Does inductance change when the frequency changes?	No. L is set by construction. Xₗ changes, because Xₗ is proportional to f. (Exception: iron cores saturate at high current)
What is a pure inductor's Xₗ at DC (0 Hz)?	Zero. Only the wire resistance limits current. (An AC coil on DC will burn up)
Raise the frequency: what happens to Xₗ and current?	Xₗ goes up and current goes down (in proportion).
60 Hz coil or transformer on 50 Hz: what happens?	Xₗ drops about 17%, so current rises about 20%. It runs hotter, and an iron core may saturate.
50 Hz magnetic ballast on 60 Hz: overcurrent risk?	No. Xₗ is higher at 60 Hz, so current is lower. The likely trouble is underpowered or poor lamp starting. (Practical Application 1)
What happens to an iron-core inductor at high current?	The core saturates, inductance drops, and current climbs quickly. Air cores don't saturate.
Unit pitfall: 33 mH in the reactance formula?	Convert to henrys first: 0.033 H. Likewise kHz → Hz. (Practice Problem 2)
What do these two schematic symbols mean?	Plain coil = air-core inductor. Coil with parallel lines = iron-core inductor. (16–3)
Total inductance of inductors in series?	Lₜ = L₁ + L₂ + L₃ (likewise Xₗₜ = Xₗ₁ + Xₗ₂ + Xₗ₃) (16–4)
Three 0.6, 0.4 and 0.5 H inductors in series: Lₜ?	1.5 H (Example 16–4)
Total inductance of inductors in parallel?	1/Lₜ = 1/L₁ + 1/L₂ + 1/L₃ (product-over-sum for two; L/N for N equal ones) (Same forms for Xₗ)
2.5, 1.8 and 1.2 H in parallel: Lₜ?	1 / (0.4 + 0.556 + 0.833) = 0.559 H (Example 16–6)
Sanity check for parallel inductance?	The total must be smaller than the smallest inductor. (Forgetting the final reciprocal is the classic error)
When do the simple series/parallel inductor formulas NOT apply?	When the coils are magnetically coupled (mutual inductance), e.g. close together or sharing a core. (Transformers work this way)
In a pure inductor, how are current and voltage related in phase?	Current LAGS the applied voltage by 90°. (16–6)
Explain the 90° lag in three steps.	1. Induced V is max when current changes fastest (at its zero crossing), so current leads the induced V by 90°. 2. Induced V is 180° from the applied V. 3. So current lags the applied V by 90°.
What does "ELI the ICE man" mean?	ELI: in an inductor (L), E comes before I (current lags). ICE: in a capacitor (C), I comes before E (current leads).
Is the lag exactly 90° in a real coil?	No. Wire resistance makes it a bit less. Loaded motors are often 30–40°.
How much true power does a pure inductor use?	Zero watts. Energy is stored in the field and returned each cycle. (16–7)
In a pure inductor, what fraction of the time do V and I have the same sign?	50%. Same sign: energy goes into the field. Opposite sign: it returns. Net zero. (Figure 16–15 A–D)
General power formula for any circuit?	P = E × I × cos θ (θ = phase angle between voltage and current)
cos θ for a resistive vs a pure inductive circuit?	Resistive: cos 0° = 1 (P = E × I). Pure inductive: cos 90° = 0 (P = 0).
Where does the power an inductor DOES use come from?	Losses: I²R in the wire, plus eddy-current and hysteresis losses in the iron core.
What are VARs?	Volt-amperes-reactive: reactive power, the power that charges and returns the magnetic field. (16–8)
Formulas for VARs?	VARs = Eₗ × Iₗ = Eₗ² / Xₗ = Iₗ² × Xₗ (Use the inductor's own voltage and current)
Are VARs harmless because they're not true power?	No. They're real current in real conductors, which heats wires and transformers. That's why reactors carry kVAR ratings.
Inductor on 480 V drawing 125 A: minimum kVAR rating?	480 × 125 / 1000 = 60 kVAR. Less than that and it would likely be destroyed. (Example 16–7)
What is the Q of an inductor?	Quality: the ratio of reactance to resistance. Q = Xₗ / R (no units). (16–9)
Q formulas, rearranged?	Q = Xₗ / R, Xₗ = Q × R, R = Xₗ / Q (Review Q11–13)
High Q vs low Q coil construction?	Large wire → low R → high Q. Many turns of fine wire → high R → low Q.
Above what Q is a coil treated as pure?	Q ≥ 10. The resistance is then negligible. (Xₗ 100, R 10 → Z = 100.5 Ω)
What is impedance (Z)?	The total current-limiting effect (R and X combined), in ohms.
Why does Z = √(R² + Xₗ²), not R + Xₗ?	R and Xₗ are 90° apart, so they form the legs of a right triangle, and Z is the hypotenuse.
480 V, 60 Hz, 0.6 A, 150 Ω wire resistance. Find L.	Z = 480/0.6 = 800 Ω. Xₗ = √(800² − 150²) = 785.8 Ω. L = 785.8/377 = 2.084 H. (Procedure: Z = E/I → remove R → L = Xₗ/2πf)
E/I measured on a coil gives you Xₗ or Z?	Z. It's only about equal to Xₗ when Q ≥ 10.
What is inductive kick?	When a coil's circuit opens, its collapsing field produces a voltage spike many times the supply voltage. (Arcing contacts; it's why coils get diodes, snubbers or MOVs)
Why discharge large windings after a DC winding-resistance or DLRO test?	The DC test current stores energy in the field, and breaking the leads can produce a dangerous kick. Let the test set discharge it.
Why must you never open a loaded CT secondary?	With nothing to limit it, the secondary voltage can rise to lethal levels. Short the secondary before disconnecting a meter or relay.
Why does a stuck-open contactor armature burn out the coil?	The air gap lowers L, so Xₗ is low and the coil keeps drawing inrush current (several × sealed current).
Why do inductive loads lower power factor?	They make current lag voltage, adding reactive current. Utilities surcharge low PF; capacitors correct it. (Preview of later units)
What are VFD line reactors?	Series inductors (rated in % impedance / kVAR) that limit inrush and fault current and smooth harmonics.
Replacement inductor: 324 V across it, 18 A, 1.2 Ω wire resistance, 480 V supply. Which voltage do you use?	The 324 V measured across the inductor, not the 480 V supply. Z = 324/18, remove R, then L = Xₗ/377. VARs from the inductor's own V and I. (Practical Application 2: compute Q to judge whether R matters)
