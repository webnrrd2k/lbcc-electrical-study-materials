Why are capacitors called the opposite of inductors?	Almost every behaviour is reversed: Xc falls with frequency (Xl rises), current leads (Xl lags), Xc → ∞ at DC (Xl → 0). (Intro)
Can current flow THROUGH a capacitor?	No electrons cross the dielectric. But on AC, current flows continuously in the circuit as the capacitor charges, discharges and recharges — it "appears" to flow through. (Review Q1)
Explain the two-tank analogy.	A reversing pump moves water back and forth between two tanks: flow never stops, but no water passes directly from tank to tank. Same as electrons charging each plate in turn. (Figure 20–2)
Why does current "appear to flow" through a capacitor?	Because the AC voltage continuously rises, falls and reverses, keeping the capacitor charging and discharging. (Summary)
Does a capacitor block AC or DC?	It blocks steady DC (after charging) and passes AC — more easily at higher frequency.
What is the impressed voltage?	The voltage built up across the plates by the stored charge. It opposes the applied voltage and limits current. (20–2)
Phase relationship of impressed and applied voltage?	180° apart.
What is capacitive reactance?	The current-limiting effect of a capacitor (its countervoltage), symbol Xc, measured in ohms.
Formula for Xc?	Xc = 1 / (2πfC) (f in Hz, C in farads) (At 60 Hz: Xc = 1/(377C))
Rearranged Xc formulas?	C = 1 / (2πf·Xc) and f = 1 / (2πC·Xc)
Two factors that determine capacitive reactance?	Capacitance and frequency — Xc is INVERSELY proportional to both. (Review Q2)
35 µF on 120 V, 60 Hz: Xc and current?	Xc = 1/(377 × 0.000035) = 75.79 Ω; I = 120/75.79 = 1.58 A. (Example 20–1)
480 V, 60 Hz, 2.6 A: capacitance?	Xc = 480/2.6 = 184.6 Ω; C = 1/(377 × 184.6) = 14.37 µF. (Example 20–2)
Most common Xc calculation error?	Leaving C in µF instead of converting to farads (35 µF = 0.000035 F). Also forgetting the reciprocal (1/x).
Xc of a capacitor at DC?	Infinite — it blocks steady DC (opposite of an inductor, whose Xl = 0 at DC).
Frequency doubles. What happens to Xc and current?	Xc halves; current doubles. (20–8)
Why does Xc drop as frequency rises?	Current is charge per second (I = Q/t). At higher frequency the same charge moves in less time, so more current flows for the same voltage. (30 Hz → 1 C/s; 60 Hz → 2 C/s)
300 Ω at 60 Hz. Xc at 100 Hz?	300 × 60/100 = 180 Ω (Practice 6)
Book table error check: 470 nF at 400 Hz?	846.6 Ω (the book's Table 20–1 says kΩ). Also 470 nF @ 1 kHz = 338.6 Ω and 750 nF @ 400 Hz = 530.5 Ω.
Phase of current vs voltage in a pure capacitive circuit?	Current LEADS the applied voltage by 90°. (Review Q3/Q4)
What does "ELI the ICE man" say about capacitors?	ICE: in a capacitor (C), current (I) comes before voltage (E).
Why is capacitor current maximum when voltage is zero?	Current depends on how fast the charge changes. At 0° the voltage (and charge) is changing fastest; at 90° it's at peak and momentarily not changing, so current = 0. (Q = C × V)
Compare phase in R, L and C circuits.	R: in phase. L: current lags 90°. C: current leads 90°.
Charge on a capacitor vs applied voltage?	In phase: Q = C × V, so charge is zero when voltage is zero and peaks when voltage peaks.
True power in a pure capacitive circuit?	Zero watts — energy stored while charging is returned while discharging. (20–5)
What do the gold and red areas represent?	Gold: V and I same sign — charging, storing energy in the electrostatic field. Red: opposite signs — discharging, returning energy.
Why does P = E × I × cos θ give zero for a pure capacitor?	θ = 90° and cos 90° = 0.
What unit is capacitor power measured in?	VARs (volt-amperes-reactive), written VARsC.
Formulas for VARsC?	VARsC = Ec × Ic = Ic² × Xc = Ec² / Xc
How are capacitive and inductive VARs related?	180° out of phase — they cancel each other. (Basis of power-factor correction)
Why do plants install capacitor banks rated in kVAR?	Their leading (capacitive) VARs cancel the motors' lagging (inductive) VARs, so the utility supplies less current and PF improves.
Q of a capacitor: formula?	Q = R / Xc, where R is the dielectric's (leakage) resistance. (Flipped from an inductor's Q = Xl/R)
Other Q formulas for a capacitor?	Q = VARsC / P, and approximately Q ≈ 1/PF (accurate when Q is high).
Is a capacitor's Q usually high or low? What lowers it?	Very high. A leaky dielectric (lower R) lowers Q.
What is a capacitor's voltage rating really the rating of?	The dielectric. (20–7)
A nonpolarized capacitor is marked in VAC. What value is that?	RMS — compare directly with the circuit's RMS voltage.
Marked VDC, WVDC or PEAK. How do you use it on AC?	It's a PEAK value: compare it with the circuit's RMS × 1.414.
How are electrolytic (polarized) capacitors rated?	Always in DC volts.
300 WVDC capacitor on a 240 V line. OK?	No: 240 × 1.414 = 339 V peak, which exceeds 300 V. (Example 20–3)
600 VDC capacitor on 480 VAC. OK?	No: 480 × 1.414 ≈ 679 V peak > 600 V. (Review Q7 / Practical App 1)
A 350 VAC capacitor is equivalent to what DC rating?	About 350 × 1.414 ≈ 495 V peak (DC). (Review Q10)
Life gain at half the rated voltage?	About 8×. (Review Q8)
Is there a standard for how capacitor voltage ratings are marked?	No — you'll see VOLTS AC, VOLTS DC, PEAK VOLTS and WVDC. Read the label carefully.
In series, how do capacitive REACTANCES combine?	They add: Xct = Xc1 + Xc2 + Xc3 (same angle). (Capacitance uses reciprocals)
In parallel, how do capacitive REACTANCES combine?	Reciprocal formula: 1/Xct = 1/Xc1 + 1/Xc2 + 1/Xc3. (Capacitance adds)
10, 30, 15 µF in series on 480 V, 60 Hz. Xct, Ct and I?	Xct = 265 + 88 + 177 = 530.5 Ω; Ct = 5 µF; I = 0.905 A. (Example 20–4)
In that series circuit, which capacitor has the largest voltage drop?	The smallest one (10 µF): 240 V. Drops are 240, 80, 160 V. (Check each cap's rating)
Do the series capacitor voltage drops add straight?	Yes — all capacitor drops are in phase with each other (240 + 80 + 160 = 480 V). (Unlike R + L drops)
50, 75, 20 µF in parallel, 787 VARs at 60 Hz. Find E.	Xct = 18.29 Ω; E = √(VARs × Xct) = √(787 × 18.29) ≈ 120 V. (Example 20–5)
Formula for E from VARs and reactance?	E = √(VARs × X)
Do VARs from several capacitors add?	Yes — same-type VARs add straight in series or parallel.
Capacitor drawn onto a higher voltage than rated: two effects?	VARs rise with V² (480 → 600 V ≈ +56% kVAR) and the dielectric is overstressed.
Why can PF capacitors overheat on VFD/electronic systems?	Their Xc drops at harmonic frequencies, so they draw extra harmonic current and can resonate with system inductance. (Detuned PF banks)
Field formula to check a run capacitor at 60 Hz?	C (µF) ≈ 2652 × I ÷ E (current through it, voltage across it).
Add a series capacitor to reduce current from 24 A to 16 A on 480 V. Method?	Existing Xc = 480/24; needed total = 480/16; series Xc's add, so the new cap supplies the difference in Xc. Convert that Xc to µF. (Practice 7)
Four equal capacitors in series must total 20 µF. Value of each?	C/N = 20 µF → each = 80 µF; each sees ¼ of the line voltage. (Review Q11–12)
