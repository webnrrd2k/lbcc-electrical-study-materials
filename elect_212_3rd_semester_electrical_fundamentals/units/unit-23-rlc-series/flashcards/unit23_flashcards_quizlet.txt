Phase of the resistor's voltage vs the series current?	In phase. (Review Q1)
Phase of the inductor's voltage vs the series current?	Eₗ LEADS the current by 90°. (Review Q2)
Phase of the capacitor's voltage vs the series current?	Ec LAGS the current by 90°. (Review Q3)
What is the phase relationship between Eₗ and Ec in series?	180° apart — exactly opposite, so they subtract.
What is the same through every part of an RLC series circuit?	The current.
How do Xl and Xc combine in series?	They subtract: net X = Xl − Xc. The smaller is eliminated, the larger reduced.
Impedance formula for RLC series?	Z = √(R² + (Xl − Xc)²) (R = √(Z² − (Xl − Xc)²))
R 12 Ω, Xl 24 Ω, Xc 8 Ω. Z?	√(12² + 16²) = 20 Ω (Example 23–1)
Xc is larger than Xl. Does the negative difference matter in Z?	No — it's squared. R 10, Xl 30, Xc 54 → √(100 + 576) = 26 Ω.
Two common Z mistakes in RLC series?	Adding Xl + Xc, or putting Xl and Xc separately into √(R² + …). Always subtract first.
Why can RLC series current be surprisingly large?	Xl and Xc cancel, so Z can be smaller than either reactance.
RLC example on 240 V: current and drops?	I = 240/20 = 12 A; Eᵣ = 144 V, Eₗ = 288 V, Ec = 96 V.
Can a component's voltage exceed the supply in RLC series?	Yes — Eₗ = 288 V on a 240 V supply. Eₗ and Ec partly cancel, so the supply only provides the difference. (Check capacitor ratings!)
Total voltage formula in RLC series?	Eₜ = √(Eᵣ² + (Eₗ − Ec)²) (√(144² + 192²) = 240 V)
L and C values in Example 23–1?	L = 24/377 = 0.0637 H; C = 1/(377 × 8) = 331.6 µF.
66.3 µF, 0.0663 H, 8 Ω on 120 V, 60 Hz. Current?	Xc = 40 Ω, Xl = 25 Ω → Z = √(64 + 225) = 17 Ω → I = 7.05 A. (Example 23–2)
Xl 62 Ω, Xc 38 Ω on 208 V. What R limits current to 8 A?	Z = 208/8 = 26 Ω; R = √(26² − 24²) = 10 Ω. (Example 23–3)
Power quantities in Example 23–1?	P 1728 W; VARsL 3456; VARsC 1152; net 2304 VARs inductive; VA 2880.
Apparent power formula in RLC circuits?	VA = √(P² + (VARsL − VARsC)²)
Why do inductive and capacitive VARs cancel?	While the coil stores energy the capacitor releases it (and vice versa) — they trade energy locally, so the source supplies only the net.
Book typo in Figure 23–6?	VARsC shown as 1142; correct value is 1152 (96 V × 12 A). Also "PF = 0.06 × 100" should be 0.6.
When is the RLC series PF lagging?	When inductive VARs (Xl) exceed capacitive VARs (Xc) — current lags.
When is the RLC series PF leading?	When capacitive VARs (Xc) exceed inductive — current leads.
Example 23–1 PF and θ?	PF = 1728/2880 = 60% LAGGING; θ = 53.13°.
18 W, 24 VARsL, 34 VARsC in series. PF, leading or lagging?	Net 10 VARs capacitive → PF = 18/√(18² + 10²) ≈ 87.4% LEADING. (Practice 6–7)
tan θ in an RLC series circuit?	tan θ = (Xl − Xc)/R = (VARsL − VARsC)/P
What is resonance?	The frequency at which Xl = Xc, so they cancel completely. (23–2)
Why must Xl and Xc be equal at some frequency?	Xl rises with frequency and Xc falls — the curves must cross.
Resonant frequency formula?	fR = 1 / (2π√(LC)) (L in H, C in F)
Formulas for the L or C needed to resonate at fR?	L = 1/(4π²f²C); C = 1/(4π²f²L) — both π and f squared. (Works for series or parallel)
C needed to resonate with 0.2 H at 1200 Hz?	≈ 88 nF
L needed to resonate with 50 µF at 400 Hz?	≈ 3.17 mH
At series resonance, what are Z, current and PF?	Z = R (minimum), current maximum, PF = 100% (θ = 0°).
0.0159 H (5 Ω wire) + 1.59 µF on 50 V: current at resonance (1000 Hz)?	Only R limits it: 50/5 = 10 A (vs ~0.47 A at 600 Hz, ~0.73 A at 1400 Hz).
In that circuit, the voltage across L and across C at resonance?	10 A × 100 Ω = 1000 V each — on a 50 V supply. (Magnified by Q = Xl/R = 20)
At resonance, do L and C disappear?	No — they cancel each other's effect on the source, but each carries full current and develops a full (high) voltage.
What is bandwidth?	The frequency range between the two points where current is 0.707 × maximum: B = f₂ − f₁.
Bandwidth formula using Q?	B = fR / Q
High-Q vs low-Q resonant circuit?	High Q (low R): sharp peak, narrow bandwidth. Low Q: flat peak, wide bandwidth. (Bandwidth is inversely proportional to Q)
Resonance at 1000 Hz, 0.707 points at 995 and 1005 Hz. Bandwidth?	10 Hz (narrow — high Q)
Resonance at 1200 Hz, 0.707 points at 1150 and 1250 Hz. Bandwidth?	100 Hz (wide — low Q)
Why can PF capacitor banks cause trouble on systems with VFDs?	Capacitors + system inductance form a resonant L-C circuit; if it lands near a harmonic (300, 420 Hz…) currents/voltages are magnified — blown fuses, failed caps. Hence detuned banks.
How do resonant AC test sets produce very high test voltages?	A tuned reactor resonates with the cable's capacitance; at resonance the voltage across the cable is magnified (≈ Q × supply).
What is ferroresonance?	Resonance between an iron-core transformer's (nonlinear) inductance and cable capacitance — often after single-phase switching — producing erratic overvoltages.
400 Hz resonant HV source: 15 mH, 2 Ω, 240 V. Capacitor voltage rating?	At resonance I = 240/2 = 120 A; Ec = I × Xc (= I × Xl ≈ 120 × 37.7 Ω). The rating must exceed THAT (×1.414 if DC-rated), not 240 V. (Practical Application)
Review Q4 (400 Hz): what must you NOT use?	377 — compute 2π × 400 for Xl and Xc.
