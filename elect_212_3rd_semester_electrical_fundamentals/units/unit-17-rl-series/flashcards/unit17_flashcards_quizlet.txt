In a pure resistive AC circuit, what is the phase relationship of voltage and current?	In phase (0°). (Review Q1)
In a pure inductive AC circuit, what is the phase relationship of voltage and current?	Current lags voltage by 90°. (Review Q2 | ELI the ICE man: E before I in an inductor (L))
In an R-L series circuit, by how much are voltage and current out of phase?	Somewhere between 0° and 90°, with current lagging. (Exactly 0° only if pure R; exactly 90° only if pure L)
What determines the exact phase angle in an R-L series circuit?	The ratio of resistance to inductive reactance (not their sizes alone). (R 30 / Xₗ 40 and R 300 / Xₗ 400 both give 53.13°)
In an R-L series circuit, how does the current compare in the resistor, the inductor and the line?	It is the same everywhere: Iₜ = Iᵣ = Iₗ. (Series rule still holds in AC. Current is the bridge between groups.)
What is this circuit, and what is its total current?	R-L series circuit. Z = √(30² + 40²) = 50 Ω, so Iₜ = 240 / 50 = 4.8 A. (The chapter's main worked example)
In a series phasor diagram, which quantity is drawn as the 0° reference, and why?	Current. It is the one quantity shared by every part of a series circuit. (Eᵣ is drawn along it (0°); Eₗ points up (90°))
Relative to the circuit current, where does the resistor's voltage sit, and where does the inductor's?	Eᵣ is in phase with the current. Eₗ leads the current by 90°. (That 90° gap is why the drops add as a right triangle)
What are the three groups of quantities in an R-L series circuit?	Resistive: R, Eᵣ, Iᵣ, P (W) Inductive: Xₗ, Eₗ, Iₗ, VARs, L Total: Z, Eₜ, Iₜ, VA, PF, θ (Chapter summary bullets)
What's the rule for using Ohm's law in an R-L circuit?	Use Ohm's law and the power formulas only inside one group (resistive, inductive or total). To cross groups, use the right triangle (the Pythagorean theorem or trig). (Book: Eᵣ = I × Xₗ and Eᵣ = I × Z are both wrong)
In every R-L triangle, which side is the resistive quantity, which is the inductive one, and which is the total?	Horizontal leg = resistive Vertical leg = inductive Hypotenuse = total θ sits between the resistive leg and the hypotenuse.
Why do the impedance, voltage and power triangles all have the same angle θ?	They're similar triangles. Voltage = impedance × I. Power = impedance × I². (30-40-50 Ω → 144-192-240 V → 691.2 W, 921.6 VARs, 1152 VA)
Define impedance (Z).	The total opposition to current in an AC circuit, combining resistance and reactance. Measured in ohms.
Formula for Z in an R-L series circuit?	Z = √(R² + Xₗ²) (Rearranged: R = √(Z² − Xₗ²), Xₗ = √(Z² − R²))
R = 30 Ω and Xₗ = 40 Ω in series. Why isn't Z = 70 Ω?	R and Xₗ are 90° apart, so they add as vectors: √(30² + 40²) = 50 Ω. (Adding R + Xₗ is the #1 AC mistake)
Name two graphical ways to add R and Xₗ.	1. Tip-to-tail: start Xₗ at the end of R. 2. Parallelogram: both start at the origin, and the diagonal is Z. Both give the same answer as the right triangle.
Two resistors and two inductors are in series. How do you find Z?	Add the R's together and the Xₗ's together first. Then Z = √(Rₜ² + Xₗₜ²). (Like quantities point the same direction, so they add straight. Practice 13)
Formula for total current in an R-L series circuit?	Iₜ = Eₜ / Z (Z replaces R in Ohm's law. Example: 240 / 50 = 4.8 A)
Why can't you find total current with Eₜ / R or Eₜ / Xₗ?	Those mix a total voltage with a single part's ohms. Only total ÷ total (Eₜ / Z) gives Iₜ.
Formula for the voltage drop across the resistor?	Eᵣ = Iᵣ × R (Example: 4.8 A × 30 Ω = 144 V)
Formula for the voltage drop across the inductor?	Eₗ = Iₗ × Xₗ (Example: 4.8 A × 40 Ω = 192 V)
Why is Eᵣ = I × Z wrong?	Z is a total quantity, so I × Z gives Eₜ, not Eᵣ. Resistive values need resistive ohms (R). (Same reason Eᵣ = I × Xₗ is wrong)
Formula for total voltage from the two drops?	Eₜ = √(Eᵣ² + Eₗ²) (Rearranged: Eᵣ = √(Eₜ² − Eₗ²), Eₗ = √(Eₜ² − Eᵣ²))
You measure 144 V across the resistor and 192 V across the coil on a 240 V supply. That's 336 V total. Is the meter bad?	No. The drops are 90° apart, so they add as vectors: √(144² + 192²) = 240 V. A meter shows magnitude only, not timing. (KVL still holds, using vector addition)
How do you find the inductance from Xₗ at 60 Hz?	L = Xₗ / (2πf) = Xₗ / 377 (Example: 40 / 377 = 0.106 H. 377 only applies at 60 Hz.)
What is true power, and what unit is it in?	The power actually converted to heat, light or work, in watts (W). Only the resistive part produces it. (P = Eᵣ × Iᵣ = Iᵣ²R = Eᵣ²/R)
Compute the watts for the 30 Ω / 40 Ω / 240 V example.	P = Eᵣ × Iᵣ = 144 × 4.8 = 691.2 W
Why can only the resistive part of an R-L circuit produce watts?	Watts are produced only when voltage and current have the same sign. Eᵣ is in phase with I. Eₗ is 90° off, so its energy is stored and returned.
Name two common WRONG ways to calculate watts in an R-L circuit.	Eₜ × I (that gives VA) and I² × Xₗ (that gives VARs).
What are VARs? Give two other names for them.	Volt-amperes-reactive: the power that charges and discharges the coil's magnetic field each cycle. Also called quadrature power or wattless power.
Formulas for inductive VARs?	VARs = Eₗ × Iₗ = Iₗ² × Xₗ = Eₗ² / Xₗ (Example: 4.8² × 40 = 921.6 VARs)
This graph shows power in an R-L circuit. What do the gold and red areas mean?	Gold: voltage and current have the same sign, so power flows to the load (or is stored in the field). Red: opposite signs, so the stored field energy is returned to the source. (Pure L: gold = red, net 0 W)
What is apparent power, and why is it called "apparent"?	Volt-amperes (VA) = Eₜ × Iₜ. It's what you get by multiplying a voltmeter reading by an ammeter reading. It looks like power, but only part of it is real work. (Example: 240 × 4.8 = 1152 VA)
Formula relating watts, VARs and VA?	VA = √(P² + VARs²) (P = √(VA² − VARs²), VARs = √(VA² − P²))
Fill in the missing side: P = 691.2 W, VARs = 921.6. What is VA?	√(691.2² + 921.6²) = 1152 VA
Book error check: Figures 17–12, 17–14 and 17–15 label the VARs as 691.2. What should it be?	921.6 VARs. 691.2 is the watts value, copied by mistake.
Two resistors (56 W and 44 W) and two inductors (152 and 88 VARs) are in series on 130 V. Find VA and I.	Watts add: 100 W. VARs add: 240. VA = √(100² + 240²) = 260 VA. I = 260 / 130 = 2 A. (Example 17–1. After that, each part: E = P/I or VARs/I, then R or Xₗ = E/I)
Can you add watts to watts and VARs to VARs directly? Can you add VA to VA directly?	Watts + watts: yes. VARs + VARs: yes (same direction). VA: only if the loads are at the same angle. Otherwise convert to W and VARs first.
Define power factor.	The ratio of true power to apparent power: PF = P / VA. Usually written as a percent. (Range 0 to 100%; can't exceed 100%)
Three ratios that give PF in an R-L series circuit?	PF = P / VA = R / Z = Eᵣ / Eₜ (any resistive quantity ÷ its matching total) (Example: 0.6 = 60%)
Why can't PF be found from current in a series circuit?	The current is the same in every part, so Iᵣ / Iₜ is always 1. (In parallel circuits it can be)
Is an R-L circuit's power factor leading or lagging?	Lagging (current lags voltage). Capacitive circuits lead. (Write "0.6 lagging" when it matters)
A plant uses 5 MW. How much VA must the utility supply at 60% PF, and at 95% PF?	60%: 5 / 0.6 = 8.333 MVA 95%: 5 / 0.95 = 5.263 MVA (Same work, ~37% less current. That's why low-PF customers pay a surcharge.)
Why do utilities charge industrial customers for low power factor?	They bill for watt-hours, but they have to generate and carry the full VA (current). Low PF means more current, more losses and more equipment capacity for the same billed energy.
Each triangle has the same VA (hypotenuse). What happens to watts and VARs as PF drops?	Watts (horizontal leg) shrink and VARs (vertical leg) grow. θ gets larger. (PF 100% → θ 0°; 80% → 36.9°; 60% → 53.1°; 30% → 72.5°)
What is angle theta (θ)?	The phase angle between the applied voltage and the total current.
How are θ and power factor related?	cos θ = PF, so θ = cos⁻¹(PF). (The book's sentence "θ is the cosine of the PF" is backwards; its math is right)
Find θ for the main example (PF = 0.6).	θ = cos⁻¹(0.6) = 53.13°, current lagging.
Besides cosine, what other trig ratios give θ?	sin θ = VARs / VA (or Xₗ / Z, Eₗ / Eₜ) tan θ = VARs / P (or Xₗ / R, Eₗ / Eᵣ)
PF = 86%. What's the most common calculator mistake when finding θ?	Typing cos⁻¹(86). Convert to a decimal first: cos⁻¹(0.86) = 30.7°. Also check the calculator is in DEG mode. (Review Q5)
Given VARs and VA (but not watts), which function finds θ?	Sine: VARs is opposite θ and VA is the hypotenuse, so θ = sin⁻¹(VARs / VA). (Review Q8)
Given θ = 22° and P = 94 W, how do you find VARs?	VARs = P × tan θ = 94 × tan 22° ≈ 38 VARs. (Practice 12)
Why does a motor winding act like an R-L series circuit?	It's a coil (inductance) of wire (resistance) with a single current path.
How does a motor's power factor change from no load to full load?	No load: mostly inductive, so low PF. As load increases, the mechanical work shows up as extra effective resistance, so PF rises. (Lightly loaded or oversized motors waste capacity)
What does the ballast do in a gas-discharge lamp circuit?	An ionized gas conducts almost like a short. The series ballast's inductive reactance limits the current, with far less loss than a resistor would have. (Fluorescent, mercury vapor, sodium vapor. Lamp ≈ R, ballast ≈ Xₗ)
Should feeders and overcurrent devices be sized from watts or from VA/amps? Why?	From VA (amps). Conductors carry the total current, including the reactive part. Sizing from watts undersizes them when PF is low. (That's why transformers and generators are rated in kVA)
Voltmeter × clamp-on ammeter gives you which quantity?	Apparent power (VA), not watts. You need a wattmeter, PF meter or power analyzer to read true power or PF.
A motor (3 Ω wire R) draws 34 A inrush on 240 V. How do you size a series starting resistor to limit it to 20 A?	1. Motor Z = 240 / 34 ≈ 7.06 Ω. Xₗ = √(7.06² − 3²). 2. Target Z = 240 / 20 = 12 Ω. Needed Rₜ = √(12² − Xₗ²). 3. Added R = Rₜ − 3 Ω. Don't just subtract 12 − 7.06. (Practical Application 1)
A 0.1 H inductor with 10 Ω of wire resistance goes on 480 V, 60 Hz. Can you ignore the 10 Ω?	No. Xₗ = 37.7 Ω, so Q ≈ 3.8 (well under 10). Use Z = √(10² + 37.7²) and I = E / Z. (Practical Application 2)
