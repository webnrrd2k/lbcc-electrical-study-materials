Why do so many real circuits contain R and L in parallel?	Every load on a branch circuit is in parallel, and mixed loads are common: lights + fan motor, heat strips + compressor. (Why You Need to Know)
What is the same across every branch of a parallel R-L circuit?	The voltage, in both magnitude and phase: Eₜ = Eᵣ = Eₗ. (Summary bullet 1)
Phase of the resistive branch current?	In phase with the voltage. (Summary)
Phase of the inductive branch current?	Lags the voltage by 90°. (Summary)
In parallel, how many degrees apart are Iᵣ and Iₗ?	90° (Review Q1)
In a parallel phasor diagram, what is the 0° reference, and why?	Voltage. It's the one quantity shared by every branch. (In series it was current.) (Iᵣ lies along E; Iₗ points down (lagging))
What sets the phase angle between total current and voltage in parallel R-L?	How the current is split: the ratio of Iᵣ to Iₗ (set by R vs Xₗ). (18–1)
In parallel, does a BIGGER resistance make the circuit more or less resistive?	Less. A bigger R draws less current, so the inductive branch dominates and PF drops. (Opposite of series.) (The branch with more current dominates)
What is this circuit, and what is each branch current?	R-L parallel. Iᵣ = 240/15 = 16 A; Iₗ = 240/20 = 12 A. (Figure 18–4)
Formula for the resistive branch current?	Iᵣ = E / R (Voltage is the same as the supply)
Formula for the inductive branch current?	Iₗ = E / Xₗ
Watts in the 15 Ω / 20 Ω / 240 V example?	P = E × Iᵣ = 240 × 16 = 3840 W
VARs in the 15 Ω / 20 Ω / 240 V example?	VARs = E × Iₗ = 240 × 12 = 2880 VARs
Inductance for Xₗ = 20 Ω at 60 Hz?	L = 20 / 377 = 0.0531 H
Formula for total current in parallel R-L?	Iₜ = √(Iᵣ² + Iₗ²) (Rearranged: Iᵣ = √(Iₜ² − Iₗ²), Iₗ = √(Iₜ² − Iᵣ²))
Iᵣ = 16 A and Iₗ = 12 A. Total current?	√(16² + 12²) = 20 A (not 28 A)
Why can't you add Iᵣ + Iₗ directly?	They're 90° apart, so they peak at different times. They add as vectors (Pythagorean theorem). (Summary bullet 4)
Iᵣ = 6.5 A, Iₗ = 8 A. Iₜ?	√(6.5² + 8²) ≈ 10.3 A (Review Q6)
Branch clamps read 16 A and 12 A, feeder reads 20 A. Fault?	No. Mixed R and L branch currents add as vectors, so the feeder reads less than the straight sum.
Two inductive branches draw 9 A and 6 A. Total inductive current?	15 A. Same-type currents are in phase, so they add straight. Then combine with Iᵣ by the Pythagorean theorem. (Example 18–2)
Easiest way to find Z if you know E and Iₜ?	Z = Eₜ / Iₜ = 240 / 20 = 12 Ω
Reciprocal formula for parallel R-L impedance?	Z = 1 / √((1/R)² + (1/Xₗ)²) (Square the reciprocals, add, root, then take the reciprocal again)
Product-over-vector-sum formula for parallel R-L impedance?	Z = (R × Xₗ) / √(R² + Xₗ²) (15 × 20 / 25 = 12 Ω)
Is parallel Z bigger or smaller than R and Xₗ?	Smaller than both (12 Ω is less than 15 and 20). (Adding a path lowers total opposition)
Can you draw an impedance triangle (R and Xₗ as legs) for a parallel circuit?	No. That only works in series. In parallel, use the reciprocal formula, product-over-vector-sum, or E/Iₜ. (Book's Helpful Hint)
Which of these gives the parallel Z for 15 Ω ∥ 20 Ω?	Only R·Xₗ / √(R² + Xₗ²) = 12 Ω. √(R² + Xₗ²) = 25 Ω is the series answer; R·Xₗ / (R + Xₗ) = 8.57 Ω ignores the 90°.
R = 24 Ω ∥ Xₗ = 20 Ω. Z?	(24 × 20) / √(24² + 20²) = 15.4 Ω (Review Q7. 31.2 Ω would be the series mistake)
Two inductors (40 Ω and 60 Ω) in parallel with a resistor. First step for Z?	Combine the inductors first with the ordinary reciprocal formula: 1/(1/40 + 1/60) = 24 Ω. Then use the vector formula with R. (Example 18–2)
Is the power triangle different for parallel circuits?	No. Watts, VARs and VA add the same way in any circuit: VA = √(P² + VARs²). (Summary bullet 6)
Apparent power in the 240 V, 20 A example?	VA = E × Iₜ = 240 × 20 = 4800 VA
How is the parallel power triangle related to the current triangle?	It's the current triangle multiplied by E (16/12/20 A × 240 V). (Same shape, same angle)
You know VA and Z but not E. How do you find E?	VA = E²/Z, so E = √(VA × Z). (Example 18–2: √(6120 × 21.176) = 360 V)
VA = 325, PF = 66%. True power?	P = 325 × 0.66 ≈ 214.5 W (Review Q8)
VA = 465, P = 320 W. VARs?	√(465² − 320²) ≈ 337 VARs (Review Q9)
PF formulas for a parallel R-L circuit?	PF = P / VA = Iᵣ / Iₜ = Z / R (80% in the main example)
Why is parallel PF Z/R instead of R/Z?	Parallel Z is smaller than R, so R/Z would exceed 1, and PF can never exceed 1. (Series uses R/Z)
R = 8 Ω, Z = 5 Ω (parallel). PF?	Z/R = 5/8 = 62.5% (Example 18–1)
Why can't voltage ratios give PF in a parallel circuit?	The voltage is the same on every branch, so Eᵣ/Eₜ is always 1. Use currents instead. (Mirror of the series rule)
Main example PF and θ?	PF = 3840/4800 = 16/20 = 12/15 = 80%; θ = cos⁻¹(0.8) = 36.87°, current lagging.
What is the relationship between θ and PF in any AC circuit?	cos θ = PF
Other trig ratios for θ in parallel R-L?	tan θ = Iₗ / Iᵣ = VARs / P sin θ = Iₗ / Iₜ = VARs / VA
Book wording check: "PF is the relationship of apparent power to true power." Correct?	Backwards. PF = true power ÷ apparent power. The book's formulas are right.
Example 18–2: R 45 Ω, Xₗ 40 and 60 Ω, VA 6120. Find E and Iₜ.	Xₗₜ = 24 Ω → Z = 21.176 Ω → E = √(6120 × 21.176) = 360 V → Iₜ = 360 / 21.176 = 17 A.
Example 18–2 branch currents? Check Iₜ.	Iᵣ = 8 A, Iₗ₁ = 9 A, Iₗ₂ = 6 A. Iₗ = 15 A, √(8² + 15²) = 17 A ✓
Example 18–2 PF and θ?	P = 2880 W, so PF = 2880/6120 = 47.06%; θ = 61.93°.
Book typo in Example 18–2: "P = 359.996 V × 8 V"?	Should be 8 A. (Also Fig 18–13's VARs labels differ slightly from the text (rounding))
Give real examples of parallel R-L loads.	Ceiling fan with a light kit; a branch with incandescent lamps, fluorescent fixtures and motors; heat-pump compressor + heat strips. (Figure 18–16)
Heat pump: 34 A compressor at 70% PF plus 10 kW strip heat on 240 V. How do you find total current?	Compressor Iᵣ = 34 × 0.7 = 23.8 A, Iₗ = √(34² − 23.8²) ≈ 24.3 A. Strip = 41.7 A resistive. Iₜ = √((23.8 + 41.7)² + 24.3²) ≈ 69.8 A. (Practical App 2. Not 34 + 41.7 = 75.7 A)
500 W of lamps + an inductive load on 120 V; total 7 A. Steps to find the load's inductance?	Iᵣ = 500/120 = 4.17 A → Iₗ = √(7² − 4.17²) → Xₗ = 120/Iₗ → L = Xₗ/377. (Practical App 1)
Adding resistive load in parallel with a motor does what to the PF?	Raises it. More resistive current, same VARs, so the angle shrinks. (The VARs don't go away.)
Where are power-factor-correction capacitors connected relative to motors?	In parallel (across the line), so their leading current cancels the motors' lagging current. (Coming in the capacitance units)
Why do NEC load calculations often add amps straight even though the physics says vector sum?	It's a deliberate, conservative simplification: the straight sum is never smaller than the vector sum.
Series vs parallel R-L: what's shared, and which PF ratios work?	Series: current shared; PF = R/Z = Eᵣ/Eₜ. Parallel: voltage shared; PF = Z/R = Iᵣ/Iₜ. P/VA works for both.
Practice 15 asks for "the reactance" when Xₗ = 48 Ω is given. What does it mean?	Almost certainly inductance: L = 48 / 377 ≈ 0.127 H. (Likely book typo)
