What is shared by all branches of an RLC parallel circuit?	The voltage. (Summary)
Phase of each branch current vs voltage?	Resistor: in phase. Inductor: lags 90°. Capacitor: leads 90°. (Summary)
How are Iₗ and Ic related in parallel?	180° apart — they subtract (cancel).
When is a parallel RLC circuit lagging vs leading?	More inductive VARs/current → lagging. More capacitive → leading.
In parallel, does the bigger or smaller reactance win?	The SMALLER reactance draws more current and dominates (reverse of series). (Xl 8 Ω vs Xc 16 Ω → inductive)
Branch currents for 12 Ω ∥ Xl 8 Ω ∥ Xc 16 Ω on 240 V?	Iᵣ 20 A, Iₗ 30 A, Ic 15 A. (Example 24–1)
Total current formula for RLC parallel?	Iₜ = √(Iᵣ² + (Iₗ − Ic)²)
Iᵣ 20, Iₗ 30, Ic 15 A. Iₜ?	√(20² + 15²) = 25 A
Can a branch carry more current than the line?	Yes — the 30 A coil branch exceeds the 25 A line current, because Ic cancels part of Iₗ.
Iᵣ 38, Iₗ 22, Ic 7 A. Iₜ?	√(38² + 15²) ≈ 40.9 A (Review Q3)
Simplest way to find Z in RLC parallel?	Z = E / Iₜ = 240 / 25 = 9.6 Ω
Reciprocal formula for RLC parallel impedance?	Z = 1 / √((1/R)² + (1/Xl − 1/Xc)²) (Subtract the RECIPROCALS of Xl and Xc)
Second impedance method (combine reactances first)?	X = Xl·Xc / (Xl + Xc) with Xc NEGATIVE; then Z = R·X/√(R² + X²). 8×(−16)/(8−16) = 16 Ω → Z = 9.6 Ω. (Positive X = inductive)
Example 24–2: R 18 Ω, 9.76 mH, 5.5 µF at 1200 Hz. First step and Z?	Xl = 73.59 Ω, Xc = 24.11 Ω (377 doesn't apply); Z ≈ 16.1 Ω. (Book's substitution shows a 74.589 typo)
Power values in Example 24–1?	P 4800 W; VARsL 7200; VARsC 3600; VA 6000.
Apparent power formula for RLC parallel?	VA = √(P² + (VARsL − VARsC)²)
Example 24–1 PF and θ?	PF = 4800/6000 = 80% LAGGING; θ = 36.87°.
PF formulas in parallel RLC?	P/VA, Iᵣ/Iₜ, Z/R (Not R/Z, not voltage ratios)
L and C in Example 24–1?	L = 8/377 = 0.0212 H; C = 1/(377 × 16) = 165.8 µF.
What is a tank circuit?	An L-C parallel (resonant) circuit, in which energy swings back and forth between the coil and capacitor.
At parallel resonance, what happens to impedance and line current?	Impedance is MAXIMUM; line current is MINIMUM. (Opposite of series resonance)
Compare series and parallel resonance.	Series: Z min, current max, voltage magnified. Parallel: Z max, line current min, circulating current magnified.
What limits the line current of a real tank circuit?	The resistance (Q) of the coil — capacitors have very high Q. (Ideal: zero line current, infinite Z)
Q of a tank circuit — two formulas?	Q = Xl/R = I(tank)/I(line)
0.0398 H (10 Ω), 0.442 µF on 480 V at 1200 Hz: tank current, Q and line current?	Xl = Xc = 300 Ω → 1.6 A each; Q = 30; line = 1.6/30 = 0.0533 A.
Check the tank line current using power.	P = 1.6² × 10 = 25.6 W; I = 25.6/480 = 0.0533 A.
Impedance of that tank at resonance?	480/0.0533 ≈ 9000 Ω (book's 9006 comes from rounding).
Tank Xl = Xc = 30 Ω, coil Q = 15, 277 V. Line current?	Tank = 277/30 ≈ 9.23 A; line = 9.23/15 ≈ 0.62 A. (Review Q4)
Bandwidth of a parallel resonant circuit?	Frequency range where impedance is ≥ 0.707 × maximum; high Q = narrow, low Q = wide.
Pitfall: "tiny line current, so L and C are lightly loaded"?	Wrong — each carries the full circulating tank current (Q × line current).
How does an induction heater use a tank circuit?	The work coil (with the pipe as core) and a capacitor resonate; I(tank) = Q × I(line) — Q 10: 100 A line → 1000 A heating current.
Induction heating: low vs high frequency?	≤ 400 Hz heats evenly through; ≥ 1000 Hz skin effect heats the surface (case hardening).
Why is an induction heater only resonant with the part in the coil?	The part acts as the coil's core, so it changes L.
How does an inductive proximity sensor use a tank circuit?	Metal near the face adds eddy-current losses, Q collapses, the oscillation stops, and the sensor switches.
C to resonate with 0.796 mH (Xl 50 Ω) at 10 kHz?	Xc must = 50 Ω: C = 1/(2π × 10,000 × 50) ≈ 0.318 µF. (Review Q5)
Why can a capacitor correct a motor's power factor?	In parallel, its leading current cancels much of the motor's lagging magnetizing current, so the line carries less.
How does a motor look electrically at no load vs full load?	R in series with L; L (magnetizing) stays ~constant, R grows with load → low PF at no load, higher at full load.
PF correction step 1–2 (240 V, 10 A, 1630 W)?	VA = 2400; PF = 67.9%; VARsL = √(2400² − 1630²) = 1761.6.
PF correction step 3–4 (target 95%)?	New VA = 1630/0.95 = 1715.8; allowed VARs = √(1715.8² − 1630²) = 535.8. (Watts don't change)
PF correction step 5–7?	VARsC = 1761.6 − 535.8 = 1225.8; Xc = 240²/1225.8 = 46.99 Ω; C = 1/(377 × 46.99) = 56.45 µF in parallel.
After correcting that motor to 95%, what's the line current?	≈ 1715.8/240 = 7.15 A (down from 10 A). The motor's own current is unchanged.
Formula for Xc from required capacitive VARs?	Xc = E² / VARsC
To correct to unity (100%), how many VARsC?	Equal to all the existing inductive VARs. (Review Q6)
Why correct to ~95% rather than 100%?	Diminishing returns — each extra point needs more kVAR; overshooting makes PF leading and raises voltage.
Does corrected PF stay constant at all motor loads?	No (despite the book). Watts drop at light load, so PF falls; an oversized capacitor can overcorrect.
Book error in PF correction step 5?	It says reduce VARs "from 1715.789" — that's VA. The existing VARs are 1761.562. Also "PF = 0679" should be 0.679.
NEC limits for a capacitor on the load side of a motor overload?	kVAR ≤ the amount to raise no-load PF to unity (460.7, avoids self-excitation); set overloads on the reduced current (460.9).
Why can PF banks cause harmonic problems in plants with drives?	Bank + system inductance form a tank circuit; near a harmonic its high impedance magnifies harmonic voltage/current. Use detuned banks.
Practical: 240 V, 15 A, 2.2 kW motor to 95%. Method?	VA = 3600 → PF ≈ 61%; VARs now = √(3600² − 2200²); target VA = 2200/0.95; VARs target; difference = VARsC; Xc = 240²/VARsC; C = 1/(377·Xc).
