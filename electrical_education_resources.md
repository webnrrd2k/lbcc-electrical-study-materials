*Instructor-style lectures with the math*

**Resources that I've found useful**

- **[Dave Gordon](https://www.youtube.com/@davegordon6819) A good mix of math, clear explanation and solid visuals. Check him out, especially for AC fundamentals.
- **[Jim Pytel](https://www.youtube.com/@bigbadtech) and [Allen Davis](https://www.youtube.com/@allendavis169/playlists) both from [BIGBADTECH](https://www.youtube.com/@bigbadtech)** Community college electrical/electronics instructors, and has full lecture series on DC and AC circuits, transformers, motors, motor controls and PLCs. They're aimed squarely at technicians and electricians, and works through the math without drowning you in it. Also free open textbooks that match his videos, including [Introduction to Motor Controls](https://openoregon.pressbooks.pub/motorcontrols/chapter/motor-controls-introduction/) and an [electronics series](https://openoregon.pressbooks.pub/electronics2/back-matter/about-the-author/).
- **[Michel van Biezen](https://www.youtube.com/@MichelvanBiezen)** has hundreds of short whiteboard videos, each a single worked problem. They cover RLC, phasors, three-phase, transformers and more. They're less polished, but excellent when you're stuck on a specific type of problem.
- **[ALL ABOUT ELECTRONICS](https://www.youtube.com/@ALLABOUTELECTRONICS)** covers more EE-level theory with clean diagrams: network theorems, AC analysis, power factor and transformers.
- **[Neso Academy](https://www.youtube.com/@nesoacademy)** has a structured network-theory course, closer to a university EE class.

**Additional visuals and intuition**

- **[The Engineering Mindset](https://www.youtube.com/@EngineeringMindset)** has excellent 3D animations of how things physically work, such as motors, transformers, three-phase power, breakers and VFDs. It's light on math, so it pairs well with Pytel.
- **[Eugene Khutoryansky](https://www.youtube.com/@EugeneKhutoryansky)** makes animated physics videos. His ones on phasors, complex numbers and electromagnetism make the "why" click.
- **[3Blue1Brown](https://www.youtube.com/@3blue1brown)** isn't an electrical channel, but his videos on complex numbers and Euler's formula are the best explanation of *why* phasor math works.
- **[ElectroBOOM (Mehdi Sadaghdar)](https://www.youtube.com/@ElectroBOOM)** is smart, an entertaining knucklehead, and not afraid to show honest mistakes. The theory under the jokes is solid.

**Trade and code skills**
- **[Mike Holt](https://www.youtube.com/@MikeHoltNEC)** is the standard for learning the NEC, which you'll need for the electrician side. He explains the reasoning behind the rules, not just the rule numbers.
- **[Electrician U (Dustin Stelzer)](https://www.youtube.com/@electricianu)** covers practical trade topics, exam prep and real-world wiring.

**Free textbooks to pair with the videos**
- **Lessons in Electric Circuits** by Tony Kuphaldt is free on [All About Circuits](https://www.allaboutcircuits.com/video-lectures/lectures/). Its AC volume covers exactly the RLC, phasor and power-triangle material in your dashboard, in plain language.
